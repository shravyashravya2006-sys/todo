How the Electoral College Works

In US Presidential elections, citizens vote for a slate of electors pledged to their candidate. Each state gets a set number of electors based on its total Congressional representation (House Reps + 2 Senators).

Ballot Chain of Custody & Verification Flow

Every single vote undergoes rigorous bipartisan monitoring from the moment it is cast to the final audit.

1

Dual-Key Sealed Boxes

Ballots are collected in tamper-evident containers requiring keys from both major party election officials to open.

2

Signature & ID Matching

For mail ballots, election workers compare the outer envelope signature against state voter registration databases.

3

Offline Tabulation

Scanning machines operate on isolated local networks with zero internet connectivity, preventing remote hacking.

4

Risk-Limiting Audits

Before results are certified, officials hand-count random samples of physical paper ballots to verify machine accuracy.

Global Election Systems Comparison

Different democracies structure executive and legislative representation in distinct ways:

Feature Presidential System (e.g., US, Brazil) Parliamentary System (e.g., UK, India, Canada)
Executive Head President directly or electorally elected by voters independently of legislature. Prime Minister chosen by the majority party/coalition in the legislature.
Separation of Powers Strict separation between executive cabinet and legislative congress. Executive ministers are active members of parliament.
Ballot Style Separate votes for Executive (President) and Representative (Congress/Senate). Voters choose local MP party candidate; winning party forms government.
Term Limits Fixed terms (e.g., 4 years). Flexible terms subject to confidence votes or maximum 5-year windows.

Helpful Answers

Frequently Asked Questions

Got questions about election day rules, voting rights, or ballot counting? Find quick answers below.

Depending on your state or jurisdiction, you may need a valid photo ID (such as a Driver's License, Passport, or State ID). Some areas accept non-photo IDs like utility bills or bank statements. Always verify your local election office rules in advance.

Under election law: If you are in line at your designated polling location before the official closing time, you have the legal right to stay in line and cast your ballot!

Mail-in ballots use a double-envelope privacy system. The outer envelope is signed by the voter. Election clerks verify the signature against the voter's official record using software or bipartisan review teams before opening the inner secrecy sleeve.

A Provisional Ballot is used when a voter’s eligibility cannot be immediately confirmed at the polling place (e.g., name missing from roster, ID missing, or address update pending). The vote is safely held and counted after election officials verify voter eligibility.
